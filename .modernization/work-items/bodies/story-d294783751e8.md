# Prove the journey: Move through the categories and policies of the lo...

## Problem

- the journey keeps working as the product changes, in the interface the team ships

## Goal

- an acceptance test that does the journey from its first step to its last, as people will do it

## Acceptance criteria

- Given the product is ready, when the test does "Decide which area to read about: user policies, computer policies or both.", then shows the policies of the chosen area; any other choice shows both, and starts with both..
- Given the product is ready, when the test does "Decide whether categories that hold no visible policies should be shown.", then shows or hides empty categories and moves the focus to the nearest visible category..
- Given the product is ready, when the test does "Move to a category and see what it holds and what it is for.", then makes it the current category, clears any chosen policy, lists its subcategories and policies and shows its description..
- Given the product is ready, when the test does "Point at a policy or a category to read what it is for.", then makes the pointed item the focus, clearing the other kind, and shows its description; pointing at nothing clears both..
- Given the product is ready, when the test does "Act on an item: open a category as the current one or open a policy for the area shown.", then offers only the actions that fit the item, takes an item chosen for an action as the focus first, does nothing when nothing is chosen, and the same opening is available without a pointer..

## Scope

- packages/contracts (the Contracts layer)
- packages/domain (the Domain layer)
- packages/database (the Database layer)
- apps/api (the API service layer)
- packages/ui (the UI components layer)
- apps/web (the Web app layer)
- apps/e2e (the End-to-end tests layer)
- packages/tooling (the Tooling layer)

## Out of scope

- Anything a later story builds on top of this.
