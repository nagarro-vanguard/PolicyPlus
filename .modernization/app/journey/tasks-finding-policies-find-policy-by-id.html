Go straight to a known category, product

Go straight to a known category, product, support definition or policy by typing its identifier.

3 steps today → 2 proposed15 rules kept · 3 dropped3 actions droppedFinding policiesProposed

Today, read from the codeModernized: Type and arrive

1Say which identifier to look for and in which area.

Offers only the areas user or computer, user, and computer, and shows status symbols as the person types; the identifier is trimmed and looked up as a category, then a product, then a support definition, then a policy, showing what kind it found or that nothing matched.

3See what was found.

Opens a visible category, opens a policy for editing and moves the list to it, shows the details of a product or support definition, or says the category is not visible or the object could not be found.

mergedName what is wanted by its identifier and arrive there.Inferred

Recognising and going are one action, saving a step.

splitRead the details of a product or support definition reached.

Details are shown only for what has them, not as a dialog every time.

2Confirm the identifier or give up.

Accepts whatever was typed, even when nothing matched, or abandons the request without a selection.

Where the cursor starts and how a window is dismissed are control details; nothing changes unless the person goes to the thing.

Why this way recommended

The system recognises the identifier and takes the person there in one step, telling them plainly when it cannot.

One step from identifier to destination, and the person is told when a policy is hidden instead of nothing happening.

Trade-off: A mistyped identifier that happens to match something takes the person there at once.

What carries it: Policy Definitions, Policy Discovery
  • Policy Definitions Support evaluator, Policy lookup
  • Policy Discovery Identifier Kind Resolver, Identifier Arrival Router, Single Selection Guard

The architecture, modernized

Rules kept and dropped: 15 kept, 3 dropped
  • Text typed in Find by ID is trimmed and looked up in this order: category, product, support definition, then policy.
  • A policy ID may end with "@U" to mean the user section or "@C" to mean the computer section; without a suffix, or with any other suffix, the policy applies to both sections.
  • When the text matches nothing, nothing is selected and a "not found" icon is shown; when the text is empty a blank icon is shown instead.
  • The area choice offers only "User or Computer", "User" and "Computer", with no free text.
  • After finding by identifier, a category opens if it is visible, otherwise the person is told "The category is not currently visible.
  • Moving to a policy first shows its category, but only when that category is visible in the tree; otherwise nothing happens and the person is not told.
  • A policy found by identifier opens in the lower-numbered of the area shown and the area found (computer is lower than user, user lower than both), so Computer wins over User.
  • Only one item in the policy list can be selected at a time.
  • After the edit dialog is accepted, which changes the open policy source in memory only, the list is refreshed in place if the current category is still visible; otherwise the program moves up to the nearest visible category.
  • Each entry of a support definition is listed by the name of the nested support definition when it is one, otherwise by the name of the product, otherwise as "<missing: " followed by the product code the entry names.
  • The product details window lists every child revision of a product with its version and name; a product with no children shows an empty list.
  • A product with a parent shows the parent name and enables the parent button; a product with no parent shows a blank parent and the button is disabled.
  • The product details window shows a version number for major and minor revisions only; for a top-level product the version is blank.
  • The product details window labels a product "Major revision", "Minor revision" or "Top-level product" from its type.
  • The support details window states how a support definition matches products: "Match all the referenced products", "Match any of the referenced products", or "Do not match products" when it has no matching logic.

Dropped
