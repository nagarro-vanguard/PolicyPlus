How it is done today

Worked out from the code, so each step says how sure we are. Marks are yours: what hurts, what is never used, what is wrong.

1. Choose which sections of policies to look at, and whether empty categories show.

Shows user, computer or both kinds of policies; shows or hides categories with no visible policies and moves to the nearest visible category.

Stated by the code

2. Pick a category and see its subcategories, policies and description.

Makes it the current category, clears any selected policy and lists its contents. Highlighting a policy or category shows its description and clears the other.

Stated by the code

3. Go into a category or open a policy.

Opens a category as the current one, or opens the policy for editing in the section currently shown; nothing happens with no selection. Enter does the same.

Stated by the code

4. See the actions that fit the thing under the pointer.

A secondary click selects a category in the tree and shows only the actions that fit a category or a policy; with no row selected in the list, nothing is shown.

Ways we propose

One place to explore

recommendedchosentask first

Choose what to look at once, then move through categories and policies as a single focus that always shows what it is for.

Costs: Merges two kinds of selection into one focus, so the product must decide what happens to the category when a policy is chosen.

Recommended because Keeps the person in one tree they already understand while removing the double selection and the up-front choices; the search variant adds a capability not shown in the analysis.

defaulted 1. Decide which area to read about: user policies, computer policies or both.

Shows the policies of the chosen area; any other choice shows both, and starts with both.

takes the place of today's 1

Why, and what it keeps

Both areas are shown until the person narrows, so no choice is needed to begin.

  • The area choice "User" shows user policies, "Computer" shows computer policies and any other choice shows both.

kept 2. Decide whether categories that hold no visible policies should be shown.

Shows or hides empty categories and moves the focus to the nearest visible category.

takes the place of today's 1

Why, and what it keeps

The same choice, kept beside the area choice so both narrow the view together.

  • The "Empty Categories" menu switches empty categories on or off and the view moves to the nearest visible category.

merged 3. Move to a category and see what it holds and what it is for.

Makes it the current category, clears any chosen policy, lists its subcategories and policies and shows its description.

takes the place of today's 2

Why, and what it keeps

Contents and description appear together as soon as a category is chosen.

  • Selecting a category in the tree makes it the current category and clears any selected policy.

merged 4. Point at a policy or a category to read what it is for.

Makes the pointed item the focus, clearing the other kind, and shows its description; pointing at nothing clears both.

takes the place of today's 2

Why, and what it keeps

One focus replaces a separate record of highlighted policy and category.

  • Highlighting a policy makes it the selected policy and clears any highlighted category; highlighting a category does the reverse; highlighting nothing clears both.

merged 5. Act on an item: open a category as the current one or open a policy for the area shown.

Offers only the actions that fit the item, takes an item chosen for an action as the focus first, does nothing when nothing is chosen, and the same opening is available without a pointer.

takes the place of today's 3, 4

Why, and what it keeps

Opening, keyboard opening and the actions offered share one rule set, and a missing selection is handled instead of failing.

  • Opening an item in the policy list does nothing with no selection, opens a category as the current one, or opens a policy's editor for the area currently shown; Enter does the same.
  • The right-click menu shows only the actions that fit what was clicked: policy-only actions are hidden for a category, and category-only actions are hidden for a policy.
  • When the right-click menu is opened from the category tree, it is shown for the selected category, and a tree with no selected category is not handled (the program fails with an error). When opened from anywhere else with a row selected in the policy list, it is shown for that row. When opened from anywhere else with no row selected, it is not shown.
  • When a category in the tree is right-clicked, it becomes the selected category, because a right-click alone does not select it; a left-click is left as is.
What this way leaves out

Fitting labels and column widths is presentation of one kind of screen; the target lays text out to suit any display.

  • Fits the description pane's labels to its width and hides its horizontal scroll bar.
  • Resizes the policy name column to fill the list.

automate first

The person says what they are after and the system narrows the categories and policies, then the usual reading and opening follow from the result.

Costs: Adds a way of finding that the old system does not have, and needs words that match names and descriptions.
